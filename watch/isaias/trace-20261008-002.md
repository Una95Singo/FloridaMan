## Addendum — expert correction (Una Singo, ~11:35 PM EDT Oct 7)

**Correction:** the draft underweighted aggregate erosion. For annual-aggregate
covers, the right question is not only "is Isaias big enough" but "how much
of the aggregate retention has prior season events already eroded."

**Finding (checked):** the 2026 Atlantic season is record-quiet — 8 named
storms before Isaias (Arthur, Bertha, Cristobal, Dolly, Edouard, Fay,
Gonzalo, Hanna), all tropical storms, zero hurricanes; Isaias would be the
season's first hurricane (strong El Niño shear). Sources:
https://www.iflscience.com/where-are-all-the-atlantic-hurricanes-in-2026-you-can-thank-an-angry-el-nino-for-the-chilled-storm-season-84794 ;
https://www.tcpalm.com/story/weather/hurricanes/2026/10/02/when-hurricane-season-ends-florida-how-many-storms-names-record-breaking/92053310007/

**Implication:** with no prior hurricane landfalls, aggregate retentions
are most likely largely intact — Isaias faces essentially full attachments
on the aggregate covers (Everglades ×2, Merna Re, Alamo Re, Cape Lookout,
Kilimanjaro agg tranches). This strengthens the "no attachment threatened"
read for aggregates. Residual uncertainty: a tropical storm can still erode
where event deductibles are low (Alamo Re carries a $50m event deductible)
— cedent-level event losses are not publicly verifiable, so erosion is
assessed as minimal, not zero.

**What changed in the hypothesis:** added the erosion assessment above;
sensitivity ordering unchanged.
