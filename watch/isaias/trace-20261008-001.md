## Exposure mapping

Method: compare the NHC forecast landfall zone (Mobile–Destin corridor,
centered Pensacola; impacts far SE Louisiana to Florida Panhandle) against
each bond's covered area. No bond is "at risk" in any actuarial sense at
this stage — this is geographic footprint overlap only, ~60+ hours before
landfall, with below-average track confidence.

**Directly in the footprint:**
- **Bayou Re 2025-1** (Louisiana Citizens; LA named storm; per-occurrence;
  att $1.05bn / exh $1.33bn): far southeastern Louisiana inside the rainfall
  outlook; wind risk concentrated on eastern parishes. Western edge of the
  forecast — a westward track shift raises relevance sharply.
- **Sanders Re II 2025-1** (Allstate; US ex-FL named storm; per-occurrence;
  attaches at $4.25bn Allstate losses): Allstate has AL/MS/LA books. But
  $4.25bn attachment needs a very large event — a Cat 2 in this corridor
  is unlikely to get there alone.
- **Merna Re Enterprise II 2026-1** (State Farm; US ex-CA multi-peril;
  annual aggregate; att/exh not disclosed): State Farm's AL/MS/LA exposure
  is real; aggregate structure means season-to-date losses count, but
  attachment level is undisclosed so no call is possible on proximity.
- **Kilimanjaro II Re 2025-1** (Everest Re; regionally-weighted
  industry-loss): any landfall generating PCS industry loss in the region
  contributes. Trigger depends on industry aggregates, not any single
  insurer's book — the right lens if the storm overperforms.
- **Everglades Re II 2025-1 / 2024-1** (Florida Citizens; FL named storm;
  indemnity, annual aggregate): Panhandle is inside the landfall zone and
  Citizens writes there. Attachments are high ($6.489bn–$15.309bn across
  tranches) — this is a severity story, not a frequency one.
- **Hestia Re 2025-1** (Kin; FL named storm; per-occurrence; att
  $405m/$605m): lowest attachments in the universe. IF the Panhandle takes
  a direct Cat 2 hit over Kin concentrations, this is the most sensitive
  position — heavily conditional.
- **First Coast Re IV 2025-1** (Security First; FL named storm; cascading
  per-occurrence; attaches above $390m/$640m): FL wind exposure; cascading
  structure adds complexity to any loss-path analysis.

**Marginal or outside:**
- **Alamo Re 2026-1** (TWIA; TX named storm + severe thunderstorm): Texas

- **Cape Lookout Re 2025-1** (NCIUA; NC named storm): not in the path.

## Hypothesis (indicative, not marks)

IF the current forecast verifies (Cat 2 landfall near Pensacola/Mobile,
late Fri/early Sat): no attachment in the universe is likely threatened.
Expected industry loss for a Cat 2 in the AL/MS/FL-panhandle corridor is
well below the attachment points of every bond in the set — the lowest
attachments ($390m–$1.05bn on First Coast B, Hestia B, Bayou Re) would
require a direct hit on populated coastline at or above forecast intensity.
The most sensitive positions, in order: Hestia Re B ($405m att,
per-occurrence, FL), Bayou Re A ($1.05bn att, per-occurrence, LA),
First Coast Re B ($390m att, cascading, FL). Everything else needs either
a stronger storm or a track shift.

Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM. The storm is 45 mph, 60+ hours from landfall,
track confidence is below average, and intensity guidance explicitly cites
dry air and shear as wildcards.

## Falsification conditions

- Track shifts >100 km west of current forecast → downgrade FL/AL
  exposure, upgrade Bayou Re (LA) and Alamo Re (TX).
- Track shifts east into the Florida peninsula → Everglades, Hestia,
  and First Coast move to the top of the sensitivity list.
- Landfall intensity below Cat 1 → "no attachment threat" confirmed;
  hypothesis stands.
- Landfall at/above forecast Cat 2 with a direct Panhandle hit →
  re-examine Hestia B and Bayou Re A against actual damage reports.
- First PCS industry-loss estimate post-landfall is ground truth for
  Kilimanjaro II's industry-loss trigger.
