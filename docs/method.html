How it works

The loop

Zero-based, the work reorganizes around a five-step loop. Agents do the assembly; a QC agent checks the mechanics; the human expert judges the hypothesis; the published trace carries everything; reality scores the predictions after the event.

1Draft
Events trigger agents — a storm being named, an advisory issued, an intensity change. Nothing starts with a human opening an app. The watch agent drafts an exposure hypothesis: trigger, inputs and sources, exposure mapping, the hypothesis, falsification conditions, and "where am I wrong?"
2QC
A QC reviewer agent validates mechanics only: source resolution, independent recomputation of every figure, units, input freshness, falsification specificity, language audit, and the expert handoff. The hypothesis itself is never QC's to judge. Verdict: PASS or BLOCKED, with named blockers.
3Expert
The human is the senior expert. The agent asks one standing question: "where am I wrong?" Corrections and decisions enter the trace verbatim as expert addenda — the expert's judgment is the scarce resource, and it compounds instead of repeating.
4Publish
The QC-passed, expert-reviewed hypothesis publishes with its full trace: every number linked to its source and its reasoning. If it isn't traceable, it doesn't ship. Superseded runs stay in the record, visibly marked, for the audit trail.
5Score
Reality is the audit. Every hypothesis ships with timestamped kill conditions — track shifts, intensity outcomes, the first PCS industry-loss estimate as ground truth. Predictions are scored against what actually happened, and the calibration record compounds.

The two kinds of wrong

A disproven hypothesis is the job working. The agent puts forward falsifiable claims with named kill conditions; reality kills some of them; that is the loop functioning. Wrong hypotheses are acceptable.

Wrong arithmetic, a stale source, or a claim without grounding is a defect — of the draft, or of the QC agent that passed it, recorded the same way. The QC agent's own sign-off says so: "If this record passed something wrong, that is the reviewer's defect, recorded the same way."

Why there is no dashboard

A better dashboard is not reimagination. Ten screens instead of twenty is not reimagination. Reimagination starts by throwing away UI/UX-driven thinking — the assumption that work happens when a human opens an application and clicks through a workflow — and asking what the work actually is.

In this work, the answer is: the interface is a chronological trace feed. The system comes to the human, and only when there is something worth their judgment. The human never "checks the system." Silence is correct: no trigger, no output. The trace is not a log for debugging — it is the shared ground truth between the agent and the expert, and the thing compliance can inspect.

See it in action: the Isaias watch feed is four runs of this loop, unedited — including the run QC blocked and the superseded drafts that keep the audit trail honest.
